The loudest attack on desktop coding orchestrators is simple: **Electron apps are slow and heavy**. cmux's launch pitch says exactly that. Superset users report multi-gigabyte renderer growth and terminal freezes. This post publishes **AO's measured numbers on one Mac**, and only cites **competitors' own public reports** for theirs. We did not install or benchmark those apps here.

## How we measured AO

Script: `scripts/desktop-perf-bench.mjs` (docs in [`docs/performance/desktop`](https://github.com/aoagents/agent-orchestrator/tree/main/docs/performance/desktop)).

- **Machine:** Apple M4, 10 CPUs, 16 GiB RAM, macOS 26.5.
- **Daemon cold start → interactive:** process spawn → `/readyz` → first `GET /api/v1/projects`.
- **Memory @ 1 / 5 / 10:** RSS of the AO daemon after N **mux-attached shell terminals** (same PTY path as session terminals; no model spend).
- **Terminal input→echo:** WebSocket mux round-trip — write a unique marker into the PTY, time until it returns in a data frame. Predictive local echo is excluded on purpose.

Raw JSON from the runs lives next to the doc. Host memory pressure made cold-start wall times noisy; memory and latency are the stable columns.

## AO numbers (this machine)

| Measurement | AO (after contained idle/buffer fixes) |
| --- | ---: |
| Daemon cold start → interactive | ~2–5 s typical on this host (8 s under swap pressure) |
| Daemon RSS @ 1 / 5 / 10 attached shells | ~30–43 MiB |
| Terminal input→echo p50 | ~51 ms |
| Terminal input→echo p95 | ~53–58 ms |

Daemon RSS did **not** climb toward gigabytes as shell count went from 1 → 10. The mux already back-pressures PTY output per connection. We also stopped two idle polling loops (settings every 15s forever; workspace backup every 15s) and capped pending terminal input queues that could grow without bound during attach/reconnect.

Electron renderer RSS and full desktop cold start need a quiet machine and `node scripts/desktop-perf-bench.mjs --electron`. We are not publishing scavenged Helper processes from other apps as AO figures.

## What competitors have reported (their words, not our benchmarks)

### cmux

cmux positions itself as a native Swift/AppKit terminal because Electron/Tauri orchestrators felt too slow. From their Show HN launch post:

> I tried a few coding orchestrators but most of them were Electron/Tauri apps and the performance bugged me.

Their product page emphasizes native + libghostty ("Lightweight: native Swift + AppKit, no Electron"). **We are not claiming cmux's millisecond startup or megabyte RSS here** — those figures appear in third-party writeups, not in cmux's own launch materials we cited. The claim we answer is the qualitative one: Electron orchestrators are assumed heavy by default.

### Superset

Superset's own GitHub issues document the class of failure people mean by "2GB memory" and "terminal freezes":

- [#3049](https://github.com/superset-sh/superset/issues/3049) — renderer RSS growing from hundreds of MB to **~2.2–3.2 GB** over ~60 minutes idle, with GC/CPU death spiral. Root-cause notes in that thread include **60fps chat display polling** (~125 IPC serializations/sec) and unbounded terminal event queues.
- [#3409](https://github.com/superset-sh/superset/issues/3409) — users reporting **~2 GB baseline** with only a few workspaces.
- [#4993](https://github.com/superset-sh/superset/issues/4993) — terminal panes **hard-freezing** under heavy concurrent agent output (PTY back-pressure / renderer not draining).
- [#4868](https://github.com/superset-sh/superset/issues/4868) — host-service V8 OOM with RSS climbing toward **~5 GB** under active terminal use.

Those are Superset's reports of Superset. AO's daemon stayed near **tens of megabytes** for 10 attached shells on this Mac in a short bench — a different workload and a different process — but it is the right order-of-magnitude contrast to "gigabytes while idle."

## What this does *not* say

- It does not say AO wins every benchmark on every machine.
- It does not say we measured cmux or Superset ourselves.
- It does not replace long-soak leak testing; the idle-poll and queue caps are aimed at the failure modes those issue threads describe.
- Chat/provider inference latency is out of scope (see also AO's separate chat-responsiveness notes under `docs/performance/`).
