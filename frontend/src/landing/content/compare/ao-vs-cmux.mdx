## Short answer

Choose **Agent Orchestrator (AO)** when you want an opinionated orchestration product: project orchestrator, worktree sessions, PR/CI/review routing, and desktop-plus-mobile supervision. Choose **cmux** when you want a native macOS terminal built on libghostty that explicitly calls itself **“a primitive, not a solution”**—tabs, splits, notifications, SSH, and a scriptable browser without locking you into an orchestrator.

These products solve different layers. [cmux](https://github.com/manaflow-ai/cmux) is a GPU-accelerated Swift terminal. [AO](https://orchestrator.inc/docs/) is a supervisor above agent CLIs and Git delivery.

## What AO uniquely has

- **Project orchestrator** and worker lifecycle across ~25 harnesses.
- **CI and review-comment routing** after a PR is open.
- **macOS, Windows, Linux** plus **iOS and Android**.
- **Apache-2.0** delivery board with GitHub/GitLab facts and an isolated browser per worker.

## Where cmux is stronger

- **Native Swift + AppKit** performance on libghostty (not Electron).
- Terminal-first UX: vertical tabs, notification rings, splits, SSH, and a **scriptable in-app browser**.
- Deliberately **no orchestrator and no PR loop**—you compose your own workflow.
- **GPL** openness for users who prefer that license family on macOS.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use AO and cmux together?

Yes. Some people run agents inside cmux while using AO for board state, orchestrator planning, and PR/CI routing—or pick one layer and skip the other.

### Is cmux an AO alternative?

Only if your bottleneck is the terminal multiplexer experience on a Mac. It is not a substitute for AO's orchestrator, worktree session model, or review automation.

### Why does cmux say it is not a solution?

Because it refuses to own task breakdown, tracker sync, or merge workflows. That honesty is useful: buy cmux for primitives, buy AO for the delivery loop.
